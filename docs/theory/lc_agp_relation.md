# Exact relation between matrix-wedge FEMPS and LC-AGP

## Evidence level

The algebraic identities below are theorem statements with executable
small-system tests. Complexity and minimal-rank separation statements are kept
distinct: an explicit expansion gives an upper bound, not a lower bound on the
minimal number of terms.

## Every even Slater is one AGP

Let `N=2M` and let

`U = (u_1,...,u_{2M})`.

Define

`Omega_U = sum_{j=1}^M u_{2j-1} wedge u_{2j}`.

Two-forms commute under the exterior product. Terms that repeat one of the
displayed pairs vanish, while every permutation of all M distinct pairs gives
the same ordered `2M`-form. Therefore

`Omega_U^M / M! = u_1 wedge ... wedge u_{2M}`.

Equivalently, with

`F_U = sum_j (u_{2j-1} u_{2j}^T - u_{2j} u_{2j-1}^T)`,

the fixed-number AGP `Psi_M(F_U)` is exactly the Slater generated by U. No
orthogonality or invertibility assumption is needed; dependent columns give
zero on both sides.

## Pathwise LC-AGP theorem

For matrix-wedge cores with bonds `chi_0=chi_N=1`, expand the virtual matrix
products. Every nonzero virtual path `alpha` emits N one-particle vectors and
therefore one decomposable Slater `Phi_alpha`. By the result above, for even N
there is a pair matrix `F_alpha` such that

`Phi_alpha = Psi_M(F_alpha)`.

Hence every even matrix-wedge FEMPS satisfies the exact identity

`C(A[1],...,A[N]) = sum_alpha Psi_M(F_alpha)`.

For dense unconstrained cores, the displayed expansion has at most

`P = product_{j=1}^{N-1} chi_j`

terms. Zeros, coincident terms, and linear dependencies can reduce the actual
count. The converse lower bound does not follow: P is not the minimal LC-AGP
rank.

The implementation exposes this identity as the deliberately exponential
oracle `materialize_femps_as_agp_path_sum`. It agrees with associative
matrix-wedge materialization on deterministic complex N=4 tests.

## LC-AGP embeds back into matrix-wedge FEMPS

A single AGP with pair rank `2r` has the ordered-channel matrix-wedge embedding
described in `pfaffian_subclass.md`, with channel bond r and
`binom(r,M)` nonzero Slater paths. A linear combination of K AGPs can be
embedded by a direct sum of their channel automata, with displayed internal
bonds bounded by `sum_a r_a` (or by a less economical diagonal Slater-path
embedding after expanding each AGP).

Thus finite LC-AGP and structured matrix-wedge FEMPS are mutually embeddable.
They differ in parameter sharing and in the cost of the chosen contraction,
not by a simple inclusion of state sets.

## Completeness makes “non-LC-AGP state” ill posed

The increasing exterior basis consists of Slater determinants, and every even
Slater is an AGP. Consequently every state in `Lambda^{2M} V_D` is an LC-AGP
with at most `binom(D,2M)` terms. At fixed N this upper bound is polynomial in
D, although its degree is N; when N and D scale jointly it is combinatorial and
is exponential near finite filling.

Therefore Phase 13 must not seek a state that is algebraically “not LC-AGP.”
Such a distinction cannot hold in the finite basis. It must seek a contraction
and representation separation in joint `(N,D,chi)` scaling.

## Correct beyond-LC-AGP gate

A candidate matrix-wedge family counts as beyond *explicit polynomial-size
LC-AGP organization* only if all of the following are established:

1. its displayed parameters and admissible bond dimensions scale polynomially
   in the declared controls;
2. contraction of norm and one-/two-body operators is polynomial jointly in
   `N,D,chi`, without enumerating `product chi_j` paths or
   `binom(D,N)` exterior coordinates;
3. the contraction uses closure or shared structure not already equivalent to
   standard Gaussian/AGP or explicit K-term LC-AGP matrix elements;
4. a genuine succinctness statement is proved or carefully bounded. Merely
   exhibiting exponentially many virtual paths is insufficient because it is
   only a nonminimal expansion upper bound;
5. the surviving distinction is checked against AGP-CI, nonorthogonal AGP,
   symmetry-adapted tensor networks, and ordered-sector first quantization.

The current finite-AGP implementation fails item 3 as a novelty criterion: it
is an explicit K-term LC-AGP calculation. It remains valuable as a stable
polynomial fallback and as the control against which a new shared contraction
must be compared.

## Odd particle number

For `N=2M+1`, every Slater is similarly a blocked AGP: select one orbital u and
pair the remaining `2M` orbitals. The same pathwise argument gives a sum of
blocked AGPs. This does not change the joint-scaling conclusion.
