# Hong et al. 2022 - Functional Tensor Network

## Core construction

An orthonormal one-variable functional basis maps a continuous wavefunction to
a coefficient tensor. Linear differential and coordinate operators become
finite matrices in that basis. For many variables, the coefficient tensor is
represented as an open-boundary MPS; norms and operator expectations become
standard tensor contractions, and the Rayleigh quotient is minimized with
automatic differentiation.

## Equations used by Phase 0

- (1)-(6): basis expansion and operator projection.
- (8)-(11): harmonic-oscillator basis, derivative, and position matrices.
- (20)-(24): many-variable coefficient MPS and one-coordinate operations.
- (27): coupled harmonic oscillator Hamiltonian.
- (29): simultaneous AD update of MPS cores.
- (31): exact two-body-coupled ground energy.

## Reproduction interpretation

Figs. 3 and 4 establish basis-order `D` and MPS-bond `chi` convergence. The
paper uses `N=4..16`, caption value `gamma=-0.5`, no three-body term, and reports
an error floor around `1e-5` for finite `D,chi`. Our first run is intentionally
smaller, then scales only after dense/native equivalence is proven.

## Phase 16 implementation correspondence

The continuous ordered-distance branch preserves the implementation-level
chain of the paper, not just its terminology:

- Eq. (6) becomes the projected matrices for the center-of-mass and positive
  distance bases. The latter are finite-interval sine functions or unbounded
  odd Hermite functions with an exact collision zero.
- Eqs. (20)-(22) become an MPS over `q=(R,r1,...,r_(N-1))`. Orthonormality
  still makes the physical norm an ordinary MPS norm, despite the coordinate
  change.
- Eqs. (24)-(26) become native local-product terms and MPO expectations.
  Mixed distance derivatives and all-pair quadratic terms are explicit
  multi-site products, while the soft-Coulomb term is a controlled polynomial
  automaton.
- Eq. (29) is retained as simultaneous global AD through latticeTN's native
  Rayleigh quotient and Adam optimizer; production training never gathers the
  `D**N` coefficient tensor.

There is one deliberate finite-basis refinement. Hong et al. represent the
second derivative by the square of a truncated first-derivative matrix. The
present code projects `-d^2/dx^2` before truncation, because in general
`P_D(-d^2)P_D` is not `-(P_D d P_D)^2` at the top basis boundary. Tests retain
both the analytic matrix identity and independent quadrature checks.

## Direct FEMPS motivation

The paper says the MPS can be generalized to fermionic tensor networks for
electronic Schrödinger equations, but gives no first-quantized antisymmetric
construction. It also observes that electronic wavefunctions necessarily have
nonzero ordinary particle-cut entropy. FEMPS tests whether this exchange
multiplicity can be made structural rather than an optimizable ordinary bond.
The ordered-distance branch answers a different question: it removes the
exchange copies by restricting to a Weyl chamber, and is therefore not the
full-space exterior construction proposed under the FEMPS name.
